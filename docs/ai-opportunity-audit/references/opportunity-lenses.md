# Opportunity lenses

The full menu of ways AI genuinely helps a small business today. Hold each lens
against the specific business during research. Most won't apply — that's fine; the
job is to *consider* all of them so you don't tunnel on time-saving and miss a
bigger win.

**Apply the lenses to THIS trade's economics — don't copy another vertical's
answers.** A lens that's a goldmine for one business is irrelevant for another.
Lens 2 (grow existing-customer value / rebooking) is huge for repeat-service trades
like cleaning or grooming, but near-useless for one-off purchases like removals or
estate agency, where the same effort should go into lens 1 (win more new leads) and
lens 3 (reputation). Before recommending from a lens, confirm it actually fits how
this trade makes money. If your recommendations would be identical for a different
trade, you've pattern-matched instead of researched — rebuild them from this
business's reality. Everything here still has to clear the no-fluff bar (real, sizeable,
buildable) before it reaches the report.

**Always build the solution — never resell a tool.** The examples below sometimes
name off-the-shelf services for illustration only; the business never recommends
signing up for them. Every recommendation is a solution you'd build (integration,
automation, custom system). Existing tools are research evidence, not proposals. If
a lens's only answer is genuinely "just buy this product", it's not an opportunity
for this business — drop it. And every build must pass the buildability ceiling in
SKILL.md: bounded, hand-over-able, not babysitting-heavy, not safety/money-critical,
not R&D. If a lens points beyond that ceiling, note it as "needs a specialist
partner" rather than quoting it. And every recommendation must be **proven** — you
found real evidence this kind of solution works for businesses like theirs. No
evidence, no recommendation.

## 1. Win more of the leads they already get
The cheapest growth: they're already paying for these leads.
- Instant auto-response to enquiries (missed-call text-back, web-form reply).
- Following up enquiries that go quiet — most owners never chase past once.
- Reactivating old/dead leads with a light touch.
- Search: "[niche] lead response time", "[niche] follow up leads".

## 2. Grow existing-customer value
Often the single biggest revenue lever for repeat-service trades.
- Automated rebooking reminders ("your carpets are due a refresh").
- Win-back campaigns to lapsed customers.
- Upsells / add-ons surfaced at the right moment (scotchguard, a service plan).
- Search: "[niche] repeat business", "[niche] customer retention", "[niche] rebooking".

## 3. Reputation that drives sales
More reviews → higher ranking → more enquiries. Direct revenue, cheap to build.
- Auto-nudging happy customers for Google reviews at the right time.
- Drafting replies to reviews (good and bad).
- Search: "[niche] Google reviews", the business's own review count vs competitors.

## 4. Marketing they've no time for
Solo owners rarely have time to market consistently; AI closes that gap.
- Social content, local SEO, website and ad copy, email newsletters.
- Search: how visible the business is locally, whether competitors out-market them.

## 5. Kill the admin (time-saving core)
- Quoting, invoicing, scheduling, diary management, payment chasing.
- The classic time-back wins; still valuable, just not the whole story.

## 6. Instant answering & triage
- Website chat or after-hours handling that books or qualifies a job while the
  owner is working. Captures business that would otherwise ring the next name on
  the list.

## 7. See the business clearly
- Turning the owner's own data into plain-English decisions: busiest/quiet days,
  best-margin jobs, which services to push. Owners often fly blind here.

## 8. Do the actual trade faster (niche-specific)
- Production help specific to the work: transcription for a therapist, drafting for
  a solicitor, listing copy for an estate agent, menu/allergen work for a caterer.
- Search: "AI for [niche] [core task]".

## How to use this in the report
Recommendations can be time-saving OR revenue-growing OR better-decisions — lead
with whichever is biggest for this business. If both a strong time-save and a
strong revenue play exist, the report is more compelling for showing both: owners
light up at "make more" even more than "waste less". Just never pad — a weak
revenue idea is still fluff and still gets cut.
